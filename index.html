From this coast

Everything Arpilo makes begins at the edge of British Columbia, where the Pacific meets stone that is older than the sea beside it. Nature keeps most of what it makes out of sight: a colony on a rock you walk past, a shell sealed in a pebble for seventy million years. We make the stone the way it is, quiet by day, and let the dark give up what is inside.

Lichen

Walk any shore on this coast and the rocks are written on. Where the spray reaches, a black crust of lichen holds the stone; a little higher come the orange and gold rosettes, and above them the grey-green maps that grow a fraction of a millimetre a year. Each is two lives in one, a fungus and an alga that agreed to share, and among the first living things to take hold of bare rock. Lichen keeps its colony in fields of small cells divided by dark lines. Ours does the same, and shows them only when the light is behind it.

Ammonite

The same shores remember an older sea. The sandstones of Hornby Island and the east coast of Vancouver Island settled on a sea floor some seventy million years ago, and they still give up ammonites: the coiled shells of animals that swam here, turned to stone. The Ammonite Slab holds one the way the rock does, a faint impression by day and, by night, the whole shell, chamber by chamber.

Nodule

Some of those shells were sealed inside nodules, rounded stones that formed around them on the ancient sea floor and kept them whole. Split one on the beach and the ammonite is there, where it has waited all along. Our Nodule is that moment, held: a stone like any other until the light inside finds what it carries.

Questions

Is it a candle?

No. There is no flame, no wax and no smoke. A small flameless LED tealight sits inside and the stone does the rest. It is designed for an LED and nothing else; the warning is embossed on the inside of every piece where you will never see it unless you look.

What light does it take, and is one included?

Flameless LED tealights only, coin-cell or rechargeable; no open flame. Two come with the Lichen, and the Lantern comes with a copper fairy string. Everything about the light.

How long does the light last?

It depends on the light: makers quote anything from a day of steady light to a hundred hours from one cell. We state the figure for the light in the box on its card once we have measured it ourselves. A rechargeable set fits the same seat if you would rather never buy a cell.

Can it go outside?

For an evening, yes: a step, a garden table, a tent. It takes a wet evening, and a lit stone in snow is the best it ever looks; bring it in afterwards. It is not made to live outdoors through a season, and it must stay out of direct sun. Heat is the only thing that can hurt it: never above 50 °C / 120 °F, so not a hot car and not a radiator.

Is it safe around children and pets?

There is no flame, and an LED tealight runs cool, so it can go on a bookshelf, a windowsill, beside a bed or into a child's room. The one thing to keep from small children is the tealight itself, because like every small light it runs on a button cell.

Why does mine look different from the photographs?

Because a phone sees light differently from your eye. In the dark it brightens the glow and shifts the colour, so a lamp can look warmer, whiter or brighter on a screen than in your room. Every photograph here is a real piece, lit with the room lights off, and cropped, nothing else.

Does it come gift-ready?

Yes, and for CAD 5 we wrap it and write your card by hand. Tissue, the seal, a care card, and nothing to assemble: the light drops in from the top and the base is already on. It can be handed over as it arrives.

How do I clean it?

A dry brush, or a barely damp cloth. No solvents, no dishwasher.
