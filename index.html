From this coast

Everything Arpilo makes begins at the edge of British Columbia, where the Pacific meets stone older than the sea beside it.

Lichen

Walk any shore on this coast and the rocks are written on: orange and gold rosettes, grey-green maps that grow a fraction of a millimetre a year. Each is two lives in one, a fungus and an alga that agreed to share. Ours keeps the same small cells and shows them only when the light is behind it.

Ammonite

The same shores remember an older sea. The sandstones of Hornby Island and the east coast of Vancouver Island still give up ammonites, the coiled shells of animals that swam here seventy million years ago. Our slab holds one the way the rock does: faint by day, and by night the whole shell, chamber by chamber.

Nodule

Some of those shells were sealed inside nodules, rounded stones that kept them whole. Split one on the beach and the ammonite is there, where it has waited all along. Our nodule is that moment, held: a stone like any other until the light inside finds what it carries.

Our lichen, ammonite and nodule lights are in the studio now. They join the shop as each one is ready.
